Investigation into low-frequency electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure and its biological effects on soft tissue integrity. Peter Cowan examines how chronic EMF exposure from electrical substations can cause collagen degradation and tendon weakening at levels regulators classify as "safe."

## Key Thesis

The San Francisco 49ers' unusually high rate of tendon ruptures may be linked to their practice facility's proximity to a massive electrical substation. The article explores non-thermal biological mechanisms by which low-frequency AC magnetic fields can damage soft tissue.

## Key Concepts

- **Non-thermal effects**: EMF damage occurs through mechanisms other than heat generation, contradicting conventional regulatory safety models
- **Collagen degradation**: Low-frequency fields can structurally weaken collagen, the primary component of tendons
- **Regulatory gaps**: Current safety thresholds may not account for chronic, long-term exposure effects
- **Real-world case study**: The 49ers epidemic provides empirical evidence of EMF health impact
