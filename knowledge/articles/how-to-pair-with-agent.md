## Core Thesis

The gap between folks who use agents effectively and those who don't is widening. The primary difference is **vision**. Without clear goals and direction, you end up simply reacting to events, staggering through prompts without a clear destination.

## Key Insight

Most people start their journey like a drunken sailor, reacting to whatever the machine spits out next. Those who go far have a clear vision for what they're building.

## The "Sitting Next to Me" Test

Imagine a capable peer sitting next to you when you approach a task. Rather than:
- Treating your agent like a glorified search bar
- Giving unrestricted, vague prompts
- Reacting to unrestricted results

Instead, specify what you want clearly and precisely.

## The Primary Failure Mode

**Under-specification.** When you give an agent an unrestricted prompt, you get unrestricted results, which are almost never what you want.

The more specific you can be, the better. Agents can solve nearly anything you can specify, but they need clear constraints and direction.

## Effective Pairing Principles

1. **Be explicit and specific** - Clear specifications lead to better results
2. **Have a vision** - Know where you're going before you start
3. **Avoid vague prompts** - Unrestricted prompts lead to unrestricted (unwanted) results
4. **Think like you're explaining to a peer** - Would you explain it this way to a colleague?
