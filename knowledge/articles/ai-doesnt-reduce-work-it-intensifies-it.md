One of the promises of AI is that it can reduce workloads so employees can focus more on higher-value and more engaging tasks. But according to new research, AI tools don't reduce work, they consistently intensify it. In the study, employees worked at a faster pace, took on a broader scope of tasks, and extended work into more hours of the day, often without being asked to do so.

This pattern can seem like a productivity win initially, but it comes with hidden costs. The intensification of work leads to unsustainable workload creep, cognitive fatigue, burnout, and weakened decision-making. The productivity surge experienced at the beginning can give way to lower quality work, increased turnover, and other organizational problems.

## Key Takeaways

- AI tools consistently intensify rather than reduce work volume
- Employees work faster, take on broader scopes, and extend work hours
- Short-term productivity gains mask long-term sustainability problems
- Workload creep and cognitive fatigue lead to burnout and poor decision-making
- Organizations need to adopt "AI practices" with intentional pauses and work sequencing
- Adding more human grounding can help mitigate negative effects
